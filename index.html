Physics notes

    In general relativity the Big Bang is not an explosion in space — it is the evolution of spacetime itself. Matter and energy tell spacetime how to curve (Einstein’s equation: every density and pressure gravitates), and spacetime tells matter and light how to move (distances stretch, light is redshifted, free particles slow down, horizons appear). Everything on this page is computed from this universe’s own expansion history — change dark energy, curvature or anything upstream and the fabric responds.

      Energy budget through cosmic time

      Inflation stretched quantum fluctuations into the seeds of every galaxy. A slow-roll model predicts the tilt ns and tensor ratio r of those seeds; the energy scale of inflation limits how hot the universe could be reheated, and Trh in turn decides which heavy particles (baryogenesis, dark matter) could ever be created thermally.

      Model predictions vs Planck + BICEP/Keck

      Why is there matter at all? A heavy particle X decays out of equilibrium and violates CP, producing a tiny asymmetry. Sphalerons — active only above the electroweak scale — convert lepton number into baryon number, but also erase any B+L. So when the asymmetry is made relative to Tsph and Trh decides whether it survives. The integrated Boltzmann equations give η = nB/nγ, which then feeds nucleosynthesis, the CMB and galaxies.

        Scan: final η vs the mass (and so the epoch) of the decaying particle

        A stable particle χ in thermal equilibrium annihilates until the expansion rate beats its annihilation rate — it freezes out at x = m/T ≈ 20–25, leaving a relic density Ωh² ∝ 1/⟨σv⟩. A weak-scale cross-section gives the observed 0.12 (the “WIMP miracle”). If the universe was never hotter than ~m/20, the particle is never in equilibrium at all. Its mass also decides whether it is cold (small galaxies form) or warm (they do not).

        Three light neutrinos decoupled one second after the Big Bang and still fill the universe: a cosmic neutrino background at 1.95 K, ~336 per cm³. Their masses (fixed by oscillation splittings plus the unknown lightest mass) turn them from radiation into hot dark matter that smooths structure; their number Neff is measured twice, by BBN at 1 s and by the CMB at 380 000 yr. A sterile neutrino could even be the dark matter.

        Neff seen twice: nucleosynthesis vs the CMB

        Symmetry breaking in the early universe can leave relics. A first-order phase transition nucleates bubbles whose collisions and sound waves emit gravitational waves; topological defects (monopoles, strings, domain walls) form wherever the new vacuum is chosen differently in causally disconnected regions (Kibble); and rare large fluctuations collapse into primordial black holes. Each is a window on physics far beyond any collider.

        First-order phase transition → gravitational waves

        Sound-wave + turbulence spectra (Caprini et al. 2016/2020) with the finite sound-wave lifetime. In the Standard Model (mH = 125 GeV) the electroweak transition is a smooth crossover: α ≈ 0, no signal — any detection would mean new physics.
